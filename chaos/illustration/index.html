About this system

How it works

Integration: flows are integrated with classical fourth-order Runge–Kutta (or Euler, to see how a crude integrator distorts the attractor) at fixed step dt; maps are simply iterated. “Hide transient” drops the first 5% of the orbit, before it settles on the attractor.

Butterfly effect: the twin starts at x₀ + δ. While the twins are close, |Δ| grows like eλt, a straight line of slope λ / ln 10 in the log plot (dashed); it saturates at the size of the attractor, when the twins are effectively unrelated.

Largest Lyapunov exponent: estimated with Benettin’s method (Benettin, Galgani, Giorgilli & Strelcyn, 1980, Meccanica 15, 9–20, doi:10.1007/BF02128236): a third, hidden orbit is kept at distance 10⁻⁸ from the reference and renormalized after every step; λ is the average of ln(d/d₀) per unit time (per iteration for maps). λ > 0 is the signature of chaos; a stable cycle gives λ < 0, a quasi-periodic torus λ ≈ 0.

Sharing: the address bar keeps the system, parameters and initial condition.
