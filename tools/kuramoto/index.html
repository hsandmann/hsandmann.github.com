How it works

Model: each oscillator i has a phase θi and a natural frequency ωi drawn from g(ω). It speeds up or slows down according to the sine of its phase difference to the others. The equations are integrated with fourth-order Runge–Kutta; noise, when on, is added after each step as √(2D dt) times a standard normal.

Order parameter: r eiψ = (1/N) Σ eiθj, the centroid of the dots on the circle (the arrow). In the mean-field model each oscillator only feels the centroid: θ̇i = ωi + K r sin(ψ − θi − α), so all-to-all coupling costs O(N) per step.

Plots: on the circle, colour encodes the natural frequency (all-to-all) or the position on the ring. The phase plot shows θi − ψ; locked oscillators sit on the curve arcsin(ω/Kr) (α = 0). The frequency plot shows the time-averaged frequency of each oscillator: the locked ones form a flat plateau, the drifting ones follow the diagonal Ω = ω at a distance.

Sweep: K is raised in 32 steps from 0 and then lowered again, keeping the state between steps (adiabatic continuation); r is averaged after a transient. Lorentzian g(ω) has the exact solution r = √(1 − 2γ/(K cos α)) (Ott–Antonsen); a uniform g(ω) jumps discontinuously at Kc = 4γ/π (Pazó).
